To the editors of Frontiers in Aging, Musculoskeletal Aging

Please consider “The sitting pause as a mechanistic probe of recoil and adaptive control in functional aging” as a Hypothesis and Theory article.

The paper addresses a mechanism that remains unresolved by prognostic sitting–rising tests or floor-practice studies: whether benefits arise from energy retained during descent, fresh muscular loading, or adaptation to changing support geometry. It proposes using sitting-pause history, independently controlled rising duration, and an active-comparator training study to distinguish these processes. Transfer to an untrained functional outcome is central to the hypothesis.

We acknowledge established work on floor-rise practice, active resting postures, elastic assistance, information/complexity and the energetic cost of postural stability, including recent work in Frontiers in Aging. The proposed contribution is their operational separation in functional aging. Thermodynamic and information concepts are stated with their physical assumptions; the paper makes no demonstrated lifespan claim. Two scientific schematics and a reproducible illustrative viscoelastic supplement accompany the draft. No new human or animal data are reported.

The author's earlier conceptual article on biological countercurvature is cited as a motivating perspective. The present framework excludes consciousness mechanisms and specifies a separate experimental aging question. The prior paper's full title and publisher link are included in the references for transparent attribution.
