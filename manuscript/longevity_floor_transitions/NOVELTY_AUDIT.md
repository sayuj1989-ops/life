# Novelty and overlap audit

Prepared 2 October 2026. This is a targeted primary-literature comparison, not a systematic review or a certification of priority.

## Verdict

The broad proposition that floor practice may aid function is already published. Elastic assistance can reduce chair-rise cost; muscular preload can produce tendon return without a preceding descent; balance has an energetic cost; and information/complexity approaches to aging have a long history. Those are foundations, not novelty claims.

The manuscript's candidate contribution is a combined identification framework: use the sitting pause as a mechanistic probe, control rising duration independently, distinguish descent-stored energy from fresh muscular preload, and test whether chronic functional transfer persists when inherited return is reduced. The active comparator and untrained outcome prevent practiced skill or ordinary exercise from being mistaken for a floor-specific aging mechanism. The search did not identify this complete package. That bounded result supports proposing it for review; it does not establish that no related study exists.

Recommended originality sentence: “We propose an operational separation of pause-dependent elastic history, fresh muscular preload, and chronic functional transfer in floor transitions.” Avoid “first,” “unprecedented,” “proves longevity,” “entropy reversal,” and “new law of aging.”

## Claims retained and excluded

Retained: a testable functional-aging hypothesis; transparent established mechanics; physical information definitions; proposed acute and chronic comparisons; explicit negative-result criteria.

Excluded: an optimal biological helix; helical growth as a demonstrated cause of scoliosis; arbitrary Okinawan daily rise counts; a causal lifespan effect; molecular cascades inferred from general mechanosensing; invalid or unresolved spine simulations; assigning caloric savings from movement entropy.

The manuscript is defensible as Hypothesis and Theory. It is not an original human outcome study, and the illustrative model is not a physiological result. Publication value rests on whether the dissociation clarifies an important unresolved mechanism, not on rebranding established mechanisms.
