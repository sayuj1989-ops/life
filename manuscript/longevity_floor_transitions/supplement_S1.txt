13   This supplement verifies the energy balance of the illustrative standard linear solid used in
14   the main manuscript. It supplies analytic segment solutions, an independent numerical
15   integration, normalized scenario outputs and the complete executable code. It contains no
16   participant data, human material calibration, fitted metabolic effect or estimated survival
17   benefit. The law is an established viscoelastic illustration; its verification is supplementary to
18   the proposed experimental hypothesis.

19   S2. Definitions and analytic segment solution
20   The equilibrium spring has stiffness k ∞, the Maxwell spring has stiffness k 1, and its dashpot
21   coefficient is c . The relaxation time is τ =c / k 1. Total deformation is x and Maxwell-spring
22   deformation is z . Force and stored elastic energy are

35   S3. Normalization and scenario grid
36   Total stiffness and deformation amplitude are set to one. Time is expressed in units of the
37   assumed relaxation time; no empirical seconds are assigned. Energy is in units of total
38   stiffness times deformation amplitude squared. The prescribed history starts at x=z=0, loads
39   linearly to x=1, holds deformation, and unloads linearly to x=0.

44   S4. Independent numerical checks
45   An independent fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration evaluates the internal state, external
46   work and passive loss, using 1,000 and 2,000 steps per nonzero-duration segment. It also
47   integrates the positive and negative parts of mechanical power. Analytic solutions initialize
48   each segment consistently. The script checks segment balances, complete-cycle balance after
49   final relaxation, nonnegative loss, numerical agreement, refinement agreement and the purely
50   elastic limit.

54   S5. Signed work, slackness and biological translation
55   Net unloading return is minus the integrated external work during unloading. Gross return
56   integrates the negative part of mechanical power separately from the positive work injected
57   during that segment. These differ when force changes sign. The linear material permits
58   compression, and 58 histories have negative net return. A tendon can become slack and is not
59   the same elastic element in compression; these histories must not be interpreted as biological
60   tendon energy recovery. A human extension requires tension-only behavior, measured
61   operating range and preload, appropriate nonlinear properties, and a mapped geometry.
62   The illustrative plot shows mechanical sensitivity, including a shaded region for negative net
63   return. No value should be relabeled as a floor-rise recoil fraction, oxygen saving or aging
64   effect. Force–deformation work does not by itself reveal whether energy originated in the
65   preceding descent or in subsequent muscle action.
